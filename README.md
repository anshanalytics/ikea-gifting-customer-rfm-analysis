## 🎯 Business Problem

IKEA Gifting is a UK-based, non-store online retailer specialising in all-occasion giftware and home décor. A meaningful share of its customers are **wholesale buyers**, not just individual shoppers, which makes "customer value" harder to interpret.

Leadership had **no structured, data-driven view** of which customers drive the most revenue or which are quietly disengaging. Retention and marketing were reactive, and spend wasn't prioritised toward the customers most likely to respond.

## 🧭 Objectives & Business Questions

**Objective:** identify the most valuable customer segments and quantify churn risk, so Marketing, CRM and Retention teams can prioritise effort and spend instead of applying one strategy to everyone.

The analysis answers:

1. Who are our most valuable customers, and how much revenue do they actually contribute?
2. Which customers have gone quiet and are at risk of churning?
3. How do customer value and retention trend month over month and year over year?
4. How do value and churn risk differ across geographic markets?
5. What concrete actions should the business take for each segment?

## 🗂️ Data Source

**Online Retail II** dataset — [UCI Machine Learning Repository](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii) (donated by Daqing Chen).

- **1,067,371** transaction rows, **Dec 2009 – Dec 2011**
- Fields: invoices, product descriptions, quantities, unit prices, customer IDs, country
- A backup copy is kept in this repository in case the source becomes unavailable.

## 🔧 Approach

```
Raw transactions ─► MySQL: extract, clean, deduplicate ─► Python: RFM scoring & segments
                                                              │
                          Power BI dashboard  ◄── churn flag (90-day inactivity)
```

1. **MySQL** – data extraction, cleaning and de-duplication into an analysis-ready transaction table.
2. **Python** – customer-level Recency / Frequency / Monetary scores and segment assignment.
3. **Churn flag** – fixed 90-day inactivity threshold.
4. **Power BI** – three-page interactive dashboard with slicers and drill-down.

Every finding is traceable back to the raw data through a documented, repeatable process.

## 📖 Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Recency (R)** | Days since a customer's most recent purchase |
| **Frequency (F)** | Number of distinct purchase transactions in the period |
| **Monetary (M)** | Total revenue generated by the customer in the period |
| **Churn** | No recorded purchase activity in the trailing **90 days** |

## 👥 Customer Segments

| Segment | Description |
|---|---|
| 🏆 **Champions** | High recency, frequency and monetary value — the most valuable customers |
| 💙 **Loyal** | Consistent purchase history with moderate-to-high value |
| ⚠️ **At Risk** | Previously valuable customers whose recency has recently dropped |
| 🌱 **New** | Recent first-time customers not yet established as repeat buyers |
| 👀 **Needs Attention** | Moderate recency but low purchase frequency |
| 💤 **Lost** | No meaningful activity for an extended period |

## 💡 Key Findings

From the dashboard (all figures as displayed in the report):

- **5,881 customers** generated about **$17M** in revenue; average customer value is **$2,954**.
- **Champions** are 1,291 customers (~22%) but contribute **~$11.8M, roughly 68% of revenue**. Average Champion value is ~$9,169 versus ~$429 for Lost customers.
- **50.9%** of customers are flagged as churned under the 90-day rule. **Lost** (1,524) is the largest segment by headcount, followed by Loyal (1,416) and Champions (1,291).
- **821 At Risk** customers (avg value ~$1,932) are the most actionable group: they were valuable and have recently gone quiet.
- Revenue grew from $0.7M (2009, partial year) to ~$8.4M (2010) and ~$8.3M (2011), flat year over year. Revenue and customer counts peak in Q4.
- Churn % peaked in 2010 (56.3%) and fell to 31.5% in 2011; the lower 2011 figure should be read with care since it reflects a fixed trailing-window flag on a dataset that ends in December 2011.

### Recommended actions by segment

| Segment | Suggested action |
|---|---|
| Champions | Protect: early access, loyalty rewards, VIP/wholesale account management |
| Loyal | Grow: cross-sell and upsell to move them toward Champion behaviour |
| At Risk | Win back: targeted reactivation offers before they become Lost |
| New | Onboard: second-purchase incentives to build repeat behaviour |
| Needs Attention | Nurture: low-cost engagement to lift purchase frequency |
| Lost | Low-cost, selective win-back only; don't over-invest |

## 🚧 Scope, Assumptions & Limitations

**In scope:** MySQL extraction and cleaning, Python RFM logic, fixed-threshold churn flag, three-page Power BI dashboard.

**Out of scope:** predictive churn modelling, product-level recommendations, real-time/automated scoring, and execution of marketing campaigns.

**Assumptions & constraints**
- Churn uses a fixed 90-day inactivity threshold for simplicity and interpretability, not a statistically derived cutoff.
- Wholesale and retail customers are not separated unless the data clearly distinguishes them.
- Analysis is based on historical transactions only; promotional calendars and seasonality drivers are not modelled.
